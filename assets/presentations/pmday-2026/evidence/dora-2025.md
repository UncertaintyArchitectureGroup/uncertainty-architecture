# DORA 2025: perception, throughput and stability

Used on slide 4. [Official summary](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report), [report entry](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/), [errata](https://dora.dev/research/2025/errata/). Checked 2026-09-21.

Source: Google LLC / DORA, [official infographic PDF](originals/dora-2025-infographic.pdf), page 1. Visual transcription, not an estimate from chart geometry.

These are **shares of respondents**, not percentages of speed or quality improvement. The infographic is image-only; these rows provide searchable text.

## Quantified delivery instability in the full report

The complete Google LLC report **v.2025.2** is now available here as an [unmodified PDF in ZIP](originals/dora-2025-v2025.2.pdf.zip) and [searchable extraction](originals/dora-2025-v2025.2.txt). It was obtained from a [public mirror](https://www.shaunabram.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025_state_of_ai_assisted_software_development.pdf) linked by [the hosting article](https://www.shaunabram.com/2025-dora-report-and-ai-capabilities-model/). Claims below come from the report itself, not the article. The official download still leads to a contact form; byte identity with that gated download has not been established. The PDF identifies itself as v.2025.2, the current version listed in the official errata.

**Figure 28, page 38:** the estimated standardized association with software delivery instability is approximately **+0.10 SD**, with an **89% credible interval of approximately +0.07 to +0.13**, for a **+1 SD difference in AI adoption**. Page 48, footnote 23, defines these units. These values are rounded readings of the published chart, not exact entries from a published coefficient table. [The extraction record](dora-2025-figure28.json) preserves the original vector coordinates and arithmetic; a rendered page was also inspected. Do not turn +0.10 SD into “10% more failures” or “10 percentage points.”

**Appendix, page 139:** delivery instability combines two operationally defined quantities: the share of changes that degrade service and require remediation (**change failure rate**), and the share of deployments that are unplanned fixes for user-facing bugs (**deployment rework rate**). These are respondent estimates in the survey, not independently collected CI/CD telemetry. See the matching [official survey questions](https://dora.dev/research/2025/questions/#software-delivery-instability).

The adoption measure combines use, reliance and trust (page 36). Page 48, footnote 20, explicitly cautions that the analysis provides observational comparisons rather than assured causal effects. The interval is credible, not a frequentist confidence interval. The methodological worked example later in the report is simulated; its coefficients are not empirical instability estimates.

The positive code-quality perceptions above and greater delivery instability concern different outcomes. Both remain visible on slide 4. The report's page 35 also repeats **2024** results (+7.2% instability per +25% adoption); those are historical and are not substituted for the 2025 estimate.

## Delivery interpretation and slide 7

The [official author summary](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report) reports a positive association of adoption with delivery throughput and a negative one with stability. Slide 4 leads with these two directions; the standardized instability coefficient remains subordinate and explicitly approximate. This survey result is not independently collected production telemetry.

Full report p.58 associates small batches with a stronger positive AI relationship to product performance and lower friction, despite potentially smaller individual-performance gains. P.81 explains Theory of Constraints and review/testing/integration queues. These motivate bounded experiments with flow practices on slide 7; they do not establish the universal effectiveness of SDD, repository intelligence or recovery drills.

## Numerical recheck, 2026-09-21

Figure 28's orange instability point and its single interval segment were checked again in the original PDF geometry and the rendered page. They support the existing **approximately +0.10**, **approximately +0.07 to +0.13** readings. The **89%** interval level is explicitly printed in the figure; **+1 SD** is defined in footnote 23. No exact numeric coefficient table was located, so the approximation signs remain essential.

Page 30 directly states **more than 80%** for perceived productivity improvement and **59%** for perceived code-quality improvement. The productivity bars print 41%, 31% and 13%; their sum of 85% uses individually rounded bins and is not an exact published aggregate. The slide therefore retains the authors' >80% statement. These numbers describe respondent shares, not the magnitude of improvement. The official errata still identifies v.2025.2 as the latest electronic report and lists no correction to these figures.
