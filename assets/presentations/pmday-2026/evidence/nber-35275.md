# NBER WP 35275

Mert Demirer, Leon Musolff and Liyuan Yang. *Writing Code vs. Shipping Code: Productivity Effects Across Generations of AI Coding Tools*. May 2026, **revised September 2026**. [Paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275) · [Official PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35275/w35275.pdf).

Slide 4 now uses the September revision, downloaded directly from NBER on **2026-09-21**. Cover and abstract identify the revision. More than **500,000** GitHub developers plus AI telemetry; matched event study, not an RCT or a peer-reviewed final publication.

## Table 6, printed p.33 / PDF p.35, weeks 21–30

| Component | Changed LOC | Commits | Releases |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Autocomplete | +234.3% | +30.2% | +9.0% |
| Synchronous agents | +957.5% | +153.2% | +19.8% |
| Asynchronous agents | +1254.9% | +60.7% | Not separately estimated |

**Figure 1, printed p.2 / PDF p.4**, labels the cumulative levels **25.5× LOC, 3.4× commits, 1.3× releases**. These are the authors' one-decimal labels, visually checked against the PDF. Sums using the rounded Table 6 entries give **25.467×, 3.441× and 1.288×**, respectively; extra digits in these sums are not extra precision in the underlying estimates. Do not multiply the components or interpret the outcome comparison as a conversion rate. Changed LOC includes additions and deletions. Repository releases cannot be split into human- and agent-authored components; the async dash is not a zero effect.

## Marketplace comparisons

**Figure 11, printed p.44 / PDF p.46; §7.2.3, printed pp.45–46 / PDF pp.47–48.** Among new monthly cohorts, usage in the first **3 months** does not keep pace with entry: iOS ratings roughly stable, Android downloads increase modestly, Chrome downloads decline. Thus “all usage is flat or lower” overstates the revised evidence.

The authors' prose gives **January 2025 → April 2026** low-usage shares:

| Threshold | Published shares | Difference using printed values |
|---|---|---|
| iOS, fewer than 10 ratings | About 78% → 87% | About +9 percentage points |
| Chrome, fewer than 10 downloads | 19% → 33% | +14 percentage points |
| Android, at most 100 downloads | About 22% → 26% | About +4 percentage points |

These are author-published whole percentages, not raw-data fractions reconstructed from chart pixels. Usage proxies are not consumer welfare or the entire installed-software market. SourceForge has a slight entry increase and is shown separately in Appendix Figure OA-17 because download counts are too small for meaningful usage analysis.
