# Changelog

All notable changes to the **Uncertainty Architecture repository and specification artifacts** are documented here.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/). Version numbers are assigned only through an explicit release decision.

Publications, talks, community discussions, and independent references belong under [`content/history/`](content/history/) rather than this release-oriented record.

### Changed

- Extended the AI-contributor control loop with explicit `agent_assistance` applicability, current-state bootstrap, scoped nested-instruction discovery, semantic maintainer corrective-feedback monitoring, durable-improvement routing, and a deterministic **checked-state** PR checkpoint. High-impact classification is now derived rather than trusted to PR prose alone: the protected repository-policy surface includes `.github/`, `AGENTS.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, and `DOCUMENT-METADATA.md`, while maintained Markdown is treated as high-impact when either the current-target or candidate version has `status: draft-normative` or `status: normative`; same-PR class or status downgrades therefore cannot disable the stronger controls. Change coupling and the agent checkpoint continue to share the same `merge-base(current target tip, head) → head` PR-owned diff. Except for Dependabot compatibility, `agent_assistance: none` now requires a head-bound target-branch global CODEOWNER marker whose author remains a current `write`, `maintain`, or `admin` collaborator with trusted GitHub association. AI-assisted attestations remain bound to diff-base identity, current target tip, head, tested merge result, PR-description digest, trusted review/inline-review feedback digest, and effective applicable `AGENTS.md` blobs. High-impact work remains Draft while repository-changing work is active; leaving Draft requires a fresh checkpoint plus current-authority `ua-agent-ready-approval` bound to exact head, tested merge SHA, PR-body digest, and canonical checkpoint fingerprint. Durable readiness verifies paginated PR-head check-runs against the exact `.github/workflows/change-coupling.yml` pull-request run, PR/head/check-suite, expected readiness job, and protected success step rather than trusting a same-named check. Retroactive approval-comment edits remain rejected. A separate target-owned **Agent protocol / trusted-base guard** is introduced for the self-modification and base-advance boundary: once its `pull_request_target` workflow exists on `main`, it executes only target-branch code, treats candidate files/text as untrusted API data, posts a status on the PR head, and on every `main` push revalidates open PRs so a changed target tip/tested merge can stale a checkpoint without a PR-head synchronize event. The bootstrap PR introducing that workflow is explicitly subject to maintainer review because target-owned enforcement becomes available only after merge. `ua-exception/pr-contract` is narrowed to bypass only a missing/malformed change-contract declaration; checkpoint, high-impact classification, target freshness, and Draft/readiness controls continue with safe agent-assisted defaults. Repository-contract mutation coverage protects both candidate and trusted-base wiring, and the previous dead `<base-sha>` protected-text compatibility marker remains removed in favor of actual current-target-tip semantics. Requiring `agent_assistance` remains an explicit compatibility migration for pre-existing open human-authored PRs. Feedback-derived persistent-guidance changes still require approval of the explained candidate rather than a generic approval gate for ordinary directly requested edits.
- Reconciled pre-publication conceptual review from **Maximiliano Armesto** across the article blueprint, long-form manuscript, and standalone adaptation: replaced the publication-facing `Linear Software` label with the research-only **Explicitly Authored Software** contrast while leaving the draft-normative glossary unchanged pending separate framework review; made explicit that the current Thinking-System wording is not LLM-specific while reopening whether fixed learned probabilistic functions and runtime judgment processes share the same release-contract property; split concrete pre-LLM historical cases from the independently validated low-consequence proportionality case; and kept the conceptual distinction between category membership, consequence severity, and proportionate control depth; and added direct STAMP/STPA positioning while preserving the broader bidirectional comparison with Simplex/runtime assurance, control-theoretic research, production ML/software engineering, risk and management systems, implementation platforms, and mature internal compositions. The review is recorded as conceptual critique and revision provenance, not endorsement or co-authorship.
- Refined the standalone Thinking Systems publication PDF for screen and print review: kept the page background white, added a restrained off-white panel and thin border around diagrams, separated the Figure 2 dimension heading from its three dimension blocks, rebuilt Figure 3 as parallel side-by-side panels with top-down flows, clarified the exogenous-Organization route and category-exit treatment in Figure 8A, and expanded the Taller acknowledgments to Christophe Kolb, Maximiliano Armesto, and Jan Rosen without changing the canonical Figure 8 semantics or readability thresholds.
- Refined the standalone publication adaptation while preserving the unfinished larger-paper boundary: restored the `Uncertainty Architecture:` title prefix, connected behavioral acceptability, epistemic status, authority, and consequential execution to the bounded-control capability argument, deferred formal lifecycle-horizon vocabulary in the earlier failure-surface bridge, restored the causal derivation of the four decision horizons before naming the UA Nested Control Lifecycle, added a concise engineering-practice conclusion before intellectual context and publication metadata, and synchronized the active Research Notes index with the renamed adaptation.
- Synchronized the long-form blueprint, manuscript, and shorter publication adaptation around the release-contract consequence of runtime Model Judgment: explicitly authored software releases an implementation of a consequential mapping specified before release, while a Thinking-System release also places into operation a judgment process that completes part of that mapping at runtime. The revision clarifies that the general changed object is the judgment-dependent situation-to-consequence mapping rather than necessarily the terminal action set; uses LLM systems as the motivating case where a small enumerated action set may still depend on judgment across a large, context-dependent space of situations, meanings, and evidence; separates behavioral acceptability, epistemic status, and authority; requires the surrounding control architecture to bound resulting system operation, reachable authority, and consequential effects rather than the model's internal judgment itself; distinguishes local Delivery realization defects from invalidated Delivery, Project / Architecture, or Organizational decision bases; and avoids claiming that conventional software had one path or that every Thinking System necessarily produces more errors.
- Reopened lifecycle-ownership research in the synthesis article and framework traceability. The paper now tests a sharper distinction between initial Organizational **assessment eligibility**; Project-owned Model-Judgment necessity, technical/design selection inside the standing Organizational business/authority basis, bounded-control/economics analysis, category confirmation, and **Project viability conclusion**; Organization-owned business outcome/basis plus **specific Bounded Research Authorization**, production Business Authorization, changed-basis, and initiative-level proceed/reshape/defer/stop decisions; and scoped technical **research-only** versus **production-capable Project Authorization**. A simpler design that still satisfies the standing Organizational basis may be selected and category-tested at Project without a second Organizational architecture approval; Organization is reactivated only when an Organizationally owned premise or initiative-level decision must change. Exogenous Organizational authoritative/business changes are treated as direct Organization inputs rather than as Runtime/Delivery evidence. Project-only production reauthorization is tested as valid only while the resulting production scope remains covered by the applicable existing Organizational Business Authorization; otherwise renewed/reshaped Organizational authorization is required before the corresponding production-capable technical baseline is issued. Coverage is the Organizationally owned business/authority/exposure/condition envelope and explicit renewal conditions, not a blanket approval of the specific technical architecture. The paper also tests whether multiple scoped Project Authorizations may coexist for disjoint or explicitly overlapping/nested scopes when precedence/interaction is explicit and material evidence can reconstruct the applicable authorization set. These semantics remain research under validation and do not change status-bearing doctrine or patterns by implication.
- Consolidated the public-article research workflow around the living blueprint and target manuscript after rechecking the former Phase 1 completion and operational-extension planning notes. The operational-extension review transferred two concerns that were only partially represented: reconstructability of the material active authorization/behavioral-control baseline across source, project, delivery, realization, model/configuration, evaluator, deployment, and fallback state; and explicit evidence that fallback/recovery paths are sufficiently independent from relevant primary/common-mode failures, available at required capacity/latency, transition correctly, and can restore an authorized state. Evidence-instrument validity/calibration was already present and remains explicit. The redundant intermediary note paths were then removed without changing the underlying research-claim state; Git history preserves both records, and the research-notes index points to the two active article artifacts.
